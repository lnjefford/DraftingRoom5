# Retirement household model

Policy: `household-2026-v2`; engine: `dr5-household-engine-3`.

The annual ledger funds spending and income taxes from income and eligible withdrawals, with ACA credits calculated from the same path's income. Unfunded obligations cause failure and carry into later years; they are deducted from total wealth. A funding solver checks withdrawal-rule boundaries and ACA eligibility discontinuities before finding a fully funded amount. Surplus cash is retained and invested. This replaces the previous two-pass tax approximation and cross-path ACA estimate.

Existing saved `finance-reference-v1` plans receive a new revision on opening the Retirement workspace, retaining the prior revision. The authorized household defaults are retirement at 45, Wisconsin married filing jointly, household size two, retained home, no pension streams, and an estimated spouse birth year of 1988 (age 38 in 2026, editable). Spouse-owned investments and their investment income are excluded; joint accounts retain their entered balance. Any actual spouse investment income could still affect real household taxes and ACA eligibility. Social Security streams have individual owners and recipient ages. New streams start at zero with an editable age-67 placeholder, not an invented benefit or a selected claiming strategy. Old documents decode with SELF income ownership and no spouse year, so ordinary codec reads never rewrite historical plans.

Spending is in today's dollars and includes gross insurance premiums for both spouses and mortgage principal/interest. Income taxes are additional. ACA credits reduce that gross spending exactly once. Each spouse stops receiving ACA at their own modeled age 65; the household remains two people for the income threshold. In the absence of separate quotes, each spouse is assigned half the household benchmark premium. Healthcare spending remains in the budget after Medicare starts; this is a conservative budget assumption, not a calculation of Medicare premiums or IRMAA. Employer eligibility, monthly enrollment and BadgerCare costs are not modeled.

Epic annual workbook projections remain authoritative. All Epic is sold at retirement and the workbook's net proceeds immediately enter a 60% stock / 40% bond index allocation (40% US stocks, 20% international, 40% bonds). The same allocation applies to new taxable deposits and surplus, independently of the initial taxable account's allocation. Its weights are reapplied annually. Existing accounts use complete classified holding weights when available, otherwise disclosed account-type allocations. Holdings never add a second balance. Epic uncertainty is limited to 1% around the workbook schedule, as requested.

Workbook capital gains and SAR income enter sale-year ACA MAGI and tax stacking. SAR income is inferred from the workbook SAR tax and its income-tax-rate assumption; a nonzero SAR tax without a positive rate is rejected. The workbook owns standalone Epic sale tax: regular-income tax is calculated on top of the sale, subtracting the standalone policy calculation so the workbook net amount is not taxed twice. This preserves the trusted workbook sale estimate; it is not an independent verification of its tax formulas.

Existing non-cash taxable investments use the authorized zero-cost-basis assumption. Cash/CD balances retain their supplied basis (or full value when missing). New purchases acquire their purchase basis. The pooled basis survives price declines and is reduced proportionally on sales. Basis is deflated with the real-dollar ledger, so inflation gains are not silently ignored. Realized capital losses carry forward. This is proportional pooled-basis planning, not tax-lot selection or tax-loss harvesting.

Home equity remains in the headline total but cannot fund spending while the home is retained. Nominal mortgage debt is deflated before subtracting it from real property value. Principal/interest is removed from spending when the loan is paid; property tax, insurance and maintenance remain in the non-mortgage budget. Sale and spending events must use the same retirement boundary.

## Policy anchors and limits

- [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-45_IRB): 2026 federal brackets, standard deductions, age additions and long-term gains. Unused standard deduction offsets gains. The temporary senior deduction is limited to its statutory years and phaseout.
- [Wisconsin 2026 estimated-tax instructions](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/TaxForms2026/2026-Form1-ES-Inst.pdf): tax brackets, income-dependent standard deduction and exemptions. [Retired-person guidance](https://www.revenue.wi.gov/Pages/FAQS/pcs-retired.aspx): exclude Social Security; apply qualifying self retirement-income subtractions.
- [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-25](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-32_IRB): interpolated 2026 ACA contribution percentages, with the 400%-FPL endpoint included. 2026 coverage uses 2025 contiguous-state FPL. The expired enhanced credit is not carried forward. Wisconsin income at/below 100% FPL does not receive a modeled Marketplace credit.
- [IRS RMD guidance](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2024-33_IRB): cohort-dependent start (75 for birth years 1960 onward); RMDs do not wait for the chosen retirement date. The age-120 divisor continues for later ages.

Future inflation-indexed tax and poverty thresholds follow assumed inflation from the 2026 anchor. Fixed statutory limits remain nominal. This is an explicit long-range assumption, not a prediction of future tax law. Wisconsin is the supported current policy. Annual age-60 withdrawal access approximates 59.5; Roth contribution ordering, SEPP/Rule of 55, itemization, investment distributions, trading tax lots, preferential exclusions, credits and survivor scenarios are outside the model. Return parameters remain modeling assumptions rather than a calibrated real-world probability guarantee.

Old Python parity goldens are retained as historical evidence, not new-engine acceptance criteria. Current acceptance tests use independent cash identities, tax anchors, subsidy cliff cases, basis recovery, separate spouse timing, real mortgage debt, migration round trips and the audit's previously failing examples.
